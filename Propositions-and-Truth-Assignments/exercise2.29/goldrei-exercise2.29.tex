Suppose that we are given a set $S$ of truth assignments with an odd numbers of elements. Let $D$ be the set of formulas of the language which a majority of truth assignments in $S$ makes true. Which of the following is always true? Give reasons for each case.

(a) For an well-formed formula $\phi$, either $\phi$ or $\neg\phi$ belongs in $D$.


This is true. Since if $\phi$ is not in $D$ that means that less than half of the truth assignments of $S$ make $\phi$ true. Therefore $\neg\phi$ results in more than half (since $S$ has odd size) of the truth assignments giving $\neg\phi$ a true truth value and granting it entry to $D$.

(b) If $\phi$ belongs in $D$ and $(\phi \to \theta)$ is a tautology, then $\theta$ belongs to $D$.

Suppose that more than half of the truth assignments in $S$ make $\phi$ true, so that $\phi \in D$.
Suppose that $(\phi \to \theta)$ is a tautology so all truth assignments in $D$ make $(\phi \to \theta)$ true.
Then the truth assignments of $S$ which make $\phi$ truth must also make $\theta$ true granting it entry to $D$. So, (b) is true.

(c) If $\phi$ and $(\phi \to \theta)$, belong to $D$, then $\theta$ belongs to $D$.

Suppose $\phi$ and $(\phi \to \theta)$, belong to $D$.
That means that the majority of truth assignments in $S$ make $\phi$ and $(\phi \to \theta)$ true.
